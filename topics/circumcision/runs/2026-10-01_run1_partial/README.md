## Cross-article patterns (judgment-based reading of the flags)

These patterns come from reading the flags in the per-article files. They are not statistics. Each one points to articles where the quoted sentences can be checked.

1. **Pro-circumcision reasoning in the article's own voice clusters in the core articles.** Most pro flags are in Circumcision controversies, Circumcision, Ethics of circumcision, Circumcision and HIV, Circumcision and law, Views on circumcision, Foreskin, Circumcision in Africa, Mohel, Khitan and Ashley Montagu Resolution. These forms recur:
   - adult heterosexual HIV RCT results (or voluntary adult VMMC) applied to neonates or to differently performed traditional procedures (F011, F022: e.g. Circumcision and HIV, Views on circumcision 143, Ulwaluko 94);
   - mechanisms (Langerhans cells, "viral entry points") stated as established, sometimes beside the article's own conflicting data (F034);
   - causal wording for observational benefits such as penile cancer, HPV/cervical cancer and balanitis "due to improved hygiene" (F032);
   - consent or integrity objections answered with complication, satisfaction or benefit data (F003: Ethics, Controversies, Views 75/98);
   - integrity or zero-tolerance positions labelled "absolutist", "ideological" or "individualistic absolutism" (F040);
   - opponents' evidence pre-discounted as anecdotal, advocacy-driven or biased, or critics characterized by origin (F026/F027);
   - the AAP 2012 "not sufficient to recommend routine" qualifier omitted where the "benefits outweigh risks" half is cited (F036).
2. **Asymmetric evidence standards.** In several core articles, harm-side claims are held to an RCT standard or discounted for self-report bias, while benefit claims from observational data are stated causally. Attribution verbs are asymmetric too: anti-side claims get "alleged" or "purported", pro-side claims get "demonstrate" or "confirm". History of circumcision criticizes historical pro-circumcision claims as correlational but does not apply the same test to modern ones.
3. **Anti-circumcision faults are fewer and concentrated.** They are in Forced circumcision (routine infant circumcision defined as "forced"; RCT-backed benefit called "unproven" in the article's voice), Forced circumcision of minors in South Korea, Meatal stenosis (only the high incidence range used for the headline; "39% vs 23%" called "doubles"), Penile subincision and the restoration articles. "Purported" is inserted into proponents' own arguments in Meatal stenosis and Foreskin restoration. In the core articles, anti-side claims mostly appear attributed and were not counted as article reasoning.
4. **FGM articles (‡).** Harm evidence is generally not disputed. Most flags are:
   - causal or is-ought overreach, including transferring findings about severe types to milder types (F032, F061, F022);
   - asides on the male/female distinction that are uncited, or that answer consent parity with severity differences (F003, F056);
   - "absolutism" labels for zero-tolerance positions;
   - political framing in the UK, New Zealand and 1985 Act articles, with contradictory causal stories about deterrence and enforcement.
   *Women unaffected by female genital cutting* is the outlier. Its body argues in the article's voice that FGC harms are overstated, and it accounts for 6 of the 18 pro-cutting/distinction flags in the ‡ group.
5. **Religious and liturgical articles** (Bible, Jesus, early Christianity, Feast, Holy Prepuce, Religion and circumcision) draw mostly neutral F004 flags: theological claims stated in the article's voice as fact rather than as tradition or belief.
6. **Technical and medical articles** (Paraphimosis, Redundant prepuce, Stapler circumcision, Circumcision surgical procedure, Phimosis) have few flags. Stapler circumcision's two neutral flags concern advocacy for one technique (summary claims of "consistently" better outcomes against mixed study details), not the circumcision debate.
7. **Structural problems (code counts and reading):**
   - Dangling citations: *Female genital mutilation* uses citation numbers with no source rows for 73 of 76 numbers; *Views on circumcision* lists 2 sources but uses 114 citation numbers, 112 of them dangling. Their citations cannot be traced from the snapshot.
   - Extraction artifacts ("…/fulltext)" fragments) appear in several snapshots (FGM in New Zealand, Forced circumcision, Prevalence of circumcision, Zero Tolerance day, Khitan, Meatal stenosis, Restoration device, Women unaffected).
   - Internal inconsistencies:
     - FGM Act 2003 penalty (14 years vs life);
     - FGM in the Gambia (2022 vs 2024 vote; "courts" vs Assembly);
     - Meatal stenosis incidence figures;
     - Religious views on FGM (Dar al-Ifta: permissible vs haram);
     - Holy prepuce (18 vs 31 sites);
     - FGM in Sudan (campaigns credited and called ineffective);
     - FGM in New Zealand (deterrence vs displacement);
     - Ulwaluko (two different names and numbers for the 2001 Eastern Cape Act);
     - Women unaffected (the lead states long-term harms as settled; the body grades the evidence very low).
   - Cross-article inconsistencies:
     - China prevalence (<1% / 2–5% / 14%);
     - South Korea prevalence (60–80% vs >90%);
     - foreskin nerve endings (1,000–10,000 vs 20,000–70,000);
     - meatal-stenosis/MBP case counts (11 vs 24);
     - San Francisco ballot measure ("Proposition F" vs "B");
     - infant UTI reduction (50–60% vs ~90%).
   None of these were resolved against outside sources.
8. **Articles with even-handed handling, or zero or one neutral flag:** FGM in India (selection bias noted for both sides), FGM laws by country (null and positive evidence both reported), Prevalence of FGM, Gishiri cutting, Paraphimosis, Redundant prepuce, Foreskin Man, Lipodermos.

## Caveats

- **Judgment calls.** Every flag is a model's reading of a sentence against an engine entry's detection cue. No gate, XNOR or logic computation was run (these are the framework's formal logic-gate checks; see [§1 of the method note](../../../../docs/METHOD_THREE_PHASE_TEST.md#1-the-framework-substance_lens)), and no confidence scores are given. A flag withdraws warrant from that sentence's inference; it does not show the claim is false.
- **Partial reading.** In each article the lead was read in full, plus a cue-scored subset of body sentences picked by `tools/sophistry_triage.py` (about a third of the body; capped at 45 sentences, or 80 for the seven largest debate articles). Roughly 30% of all sentences were read closely, so unread sentences may contain more flags. Each file states its coverage.
- **Counts are not rates.** Flag totals scale with article length, the share read, and how much of the article argues in its own voice. Cross-article comparisons are indicative only.
- **Code counts** (sentences, uncited sentences, dangling citation numbers, term hits) come from `tools/sophistry_counts.py` with published term lists in `tools/term_lists/`. They prompt reading; they are not evidence of bias. Hedge counts in particular are noisy (e.g. "rather", "often").
- **Attributed vs article voice.** Positions explicitly attributed to proponents, opponents or organizations were generally not flagged as the article's reasoning. Where that boundary falls is itself a judgment call.
- **No fact-checking.** Some flags note "claim needs source check" with no verdict.
- **Side labels in ‡ articles** follow the meaning given above the table, so totals mixing ‡ and other articles combine two related meanings. The totals list them separately.
