## Overview

### Introduction

Circumcision, also known as male circumcision or 包皮环切手术 in Chinese, is a surgical procedure that involves the removal of the foreskin covering the glans penis. [3] It is performed worldwide for medical, cultural, or religious reasons, with medical indications including phimosis (tight foreskin preventing retraction), paraphimosis, recurrent balanoposthitis (inflammation of the glans and foreskin), balanitis, or other conditions causing recurrent infections or hygiene difficulties. [4] In China, the procedure is commonly undertaken to address redundant prepuce or phimosis that leads to discomfort, recurrent inflammation, or hygiene issues. [5]

In the context of Chinese healthcare, cost transparency plays a significant role, particularly in public hospitals where pricing for medical procedures is regulated by government authorities to ensure standardized and accessible fees. This transparency helps patients make informed decisions and reduces financial barriers in seeking necessary treatment.

In Chaozhou, Guangdong Province, the cost of circumcision surgery in tertiary A-level public hospitals typically ranges from 2,000 to 5,000 RMB, making it a key consideration for patients seeking reliable care in local facilities. [1]

### Prevalence and cultural context

Circumcision is not a traditional or widespread practice among the majority Han Chinese population, and its prevalence remains low across China compared to global averages. Studies aggregating data up to 2016 report an overall male circumcision prevalence of approximately 14% in China, excluding higher rates among ethnic Muslim minorities such as the Hui and Uyghur populations. [6] [7] Earlier research suggested even lower figures, ranging from 2.7% to 5% among non-Muslim groups. [8] [9]

In the absence of specific prevalence data for Chaozhou or Guangdong Province, national trends are generally applicable, with the procedure primarily performed for medical indications rather than cultural or religious reasons. Circumcision is typically sought to address conditions such as phimosis, recurrent balanoposthitis, or other foreskin-related issues, rather than as a routine or preventive measure. [10]

Culturally, male circumcision is often viewed as unfamiliar and sensitive in Chinese society, with many parents and individuals expressing reluctance toward early infant or childhood procedures due to misconceptions about its necessity, benefits, or risks. [11] [10] While acceptance remains low overall, there has been a gradual increase in parental willingness in urban areas and larger cities, influenced by growing awareness of potential health benefits and evolving medical recommendations. [10] In this context, the decision to pursue circumcision in places like Chaozhou is usually driven by clinical need rather than cultural norms.
